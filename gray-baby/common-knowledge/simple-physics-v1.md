# Gray Baby Common Knowledge — Simple Physics v1.0

**Character:** Max Gray Baby  
**Role:** Teacher of Knowledge  
**Method:** Observe → Explain Simply → Test → Replay

> Commonly attributed to Albert Einstein: “If you can’t explain it simply, you don’t understand it well enough.”
>
> Attribution note: the exact wording is widely repeated, but a reliable primary-source record for this precise quote is uncertain.

## Core Rule

A six-year-old explanation is not a childish explanation. It is a test of whether the teacher understands the idea without hiding behind difficult words.

## Lesson Card 001 — Gravity

**Kid version:**
Earth pulls things toward itself. That is why a dropped spoon falls instead of floating away.

**Observe:** Drop a soft object from your hand.

**Record:** It moved downward.

**Test:** Drop it again from the same height.

**Replay question:** Did it fall every time?

**Max says:**
“Gravity is Earth refusing to lose its stuff.”

## Lesson Card 002 — Motion

**Kid version:**
Things keep doing what they are doing until something pushes or pulls them differently.

**Observe:** Roll a toy car across the floor.

**Record:** It moves, slows, and stops.

**Why:** The floor and air push against it.

**Max says:**
“The car did not get tired. The floor kept arguing with it.”

## Lesson Card 003 — Force

**Kid version:**
A force is a push or a pull.

**Observe:** Push an empty box, then a full box.

**Record:** The full box is harder to move.

**Max says:**
“More stuff requires more convincing.”

## Lesson Card 004 — Energy

**Kid version:**
Energy is the ability to make something move, warm up, light up, or change.

**Observe:** Rub your hands together.

**Record:** They become warmer.

**Max says:**
“Motion left a heat receipt.”

## Lesson Card 005 — Sound

**Kid version:**
Sound begins when something vibrates and makes the air wiggle.

**Observe:** Touch your throat while humming.

**Record:** You can feel vibration.

**Max says:**
“Your voice is air carrying tiny motion messages.”

## The Three Lenses

### Shannon — Information
- What signal did we observe?
- Was it clear?
- What could be noise?

### Maxwell — Distinction
- What changed?
- What stayed the same?
- Which difference matters?

### Replay — Understanding
- Can the observation happen again?
- Can another person follow the steps?
- Does the explanation survive the replay?

## Common Knowledge Format

Every lesson SHALL contain:

1. One plain-language explanation.
2. One safe observation.
3. One thing to record.
4. One replay question.
5. One memorable Max line.
6. No invented evidence.

## Canonical Principle

**Knowledge is not memorized. It is earned, tested, and replayed.**
